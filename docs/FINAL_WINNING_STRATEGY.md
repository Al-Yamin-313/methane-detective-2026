## 2. Direct Answers to the 13 Strategic Product Questions

### Q1: Is METHANE DETECTIVE genuinely easy to use?
**YES.** A user can reach an interactive, scientifically verified time-series trend with an estimated annual slope in **two clicks and under 15 seconds**. The 4-Question Answer Cards remove the burden of decoding raw axis numbers.

### Q2: Could a beginner independently complete a meaningful investigation?
**YES, but with minor terminology friction.** A beginner can easily select a sphere, pick a target, and see whether the trend is rising, falling, or cyclical. However, encountering terms like `ppm·m`, `XCH4`, or `Theil-Sen` without inline tooltips causes temporary hesitation.

### Q3: Is there an adequate Learning Center?
**NO.** Currently, learning content is either buried inside an accordion labeled "For Researchers" at the bottom of the page or stored in markdown files inside `docs/`. An integrated, top-level Learning Center is urgently needed.

### Q4: Can users understand scientific terminology without outside research?
**Partially.** The plain-language banners ("Monotonic Null Not Rejected", "Cyclical Monsoon") explain the *verdict*, but the mathematical metrics ($S$, $\text{Var}(S)$, $Z$, Theil-Sen) require prior knowledge or external search.

### Q5: Does the application explain how results can be useful?
**YES for methane emissions; PARTIALLY for precipitation and rivers.** The Climate Impact simulator provides real-world vehicle, home, and energy cost equivalents for methane, but does not provide equivalent real-world diagnostic guidance for drought or river flood crests.

### Q6: Does the application offer real value beyond charts and maps?
**YES, decisively.** The value lies in its **live statistical inference engine**: executing exact permutation tests, multiset tie enumeration, and Hirsch Seasonal Mann-Kendall client-side, combined with its reusable NASA Giovanni CSV ingestion engine.

### Q7: Would students choose it over existing platforms?
**YES.** A high school or university student faced with an environmental science homework assignment will choose Methane Detective over NASA Giovanni every time because it produces an instant, understandable result without queue wait times or complex data coding.

### Q8: Would researchers use it in their actual workflow?
**YES, for rapid exploratory screening.** Researchers will not use it to replace supercomputers, but they will use it to quickly test a satellite time series for non-parametric significance before writing complex scripts.

### Q9: Is daily usage realistic?
**NO.** Daily usage is a false metric for remote sensing trend tools. Natural usage is **weekly during university semesters, project-driven during investigations, and event-driven during industrial blowouts**.

### Q10: What would make users return?
**Shareable investigation URLs (permalinks), one-click CSV/image exports, and structured classroom lab assignments.**

### Q11: Should the product replace or complement existing NASA tools?
**COMPLEMENT.** Methane Detective is an **educational and analytical interpretation layer** that sits on top of NASA's open data infrastructure.

### Q12: What is the single strongest reason a person would choose it?
**Speed to Truth:** Getting an exact non-parametric trend test, verified annual slope, and plain-language explanation in under 15 seconds without writing code or waiting in server queues.

### Q13: What is the single strongest reason they might abandon it?
**Content Exhaustion:** If they explore the pre-loaded examples and do not realize they can ingest their own data via the Giovanni CSV importer.
